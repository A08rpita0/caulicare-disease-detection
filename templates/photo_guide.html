{{ icon('camera') }} Photo Guide

How to take a good leaf photo

The model learned from close-up photos of single cauliflower leaves. Photos taken the same way give the most reliable results, and poor photos are refused by the quality check.

{{ icon('sun') }}

Use good light

Natural daylight or even indoor light. Avoid direct midday sun that causes glare, and avoid very dark shade.

{{ icon('leaf') }}

Keep the leaf visible

Photograph one leaf so it fills most of the frame. Don't cover it with fingers, labels or other leaves.

{{ icon('eye') }}

Stay in focus

Hold the phone steady, tap the leaf to focus, and retake the photo if it looks blurry.

{{ icon('scan') }}

Get reasonably close

Close enough to see spots, lesions or discolouration clearly, but with the whole affected area in view.

{{ icon('image') }}

Simple background

Avoid busy backgrounds, people, tools or packaging in the frame. A plain background works best.

{{ icon('upload') }}

Upload the original

Upload the photo itself (JPG, PNG or WEBP), not a screenshot or a heavily filtered or edited copy.

{{ icon('check') }} Suitable photo

  • {{ icon('check') }}A single cauliflower leaf filling most of the frame
  • {{ icon('check') }}Sharp and evenly lit
  • {{ icon('check') }}Symptoms (spots, lesions, discolouration) clearly visible
  • {{ icon('check') }}At least a few hundred pixels on each side

{{ icon('x') }} Will be refused or unreliable

  • {{ icon('x') }}Blurry, very dark or overexposed photos
  • {{ icon('x') }}Very small images (under 128 px on a side)
  • {{ icon('x') }}Images without a leaf: people, objects, food, screenshots, blank frames
  • {{ icon('x') }}Leaves of other crops: the leaf check cannot reliably tell them apart, and the model will still pick a cauliflower class
