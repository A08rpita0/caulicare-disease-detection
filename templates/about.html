About CauliCare and its model

CauliCare screens photos of cauliflower leaves for three diseases and healthy leaves, explains each prediction with Grad-CAM, and pairs it with curated disease information. It is a portfolio and learning project, not a certified diagnostic tool.

Uncertainty

Results below 80% are labelled Uncertain (on validation, fewer than half of these were correct), 80–95% Moderate, and 95% or more High. Even High-confidence results can be wrong: the average top score on the test set was 96.8% while accuracy was {{ '%.1f'|format(m.accuracy * 100) }}%.

{{ icon('eye') }}

Grad-CAM

Gradients of the predicted class score (taken before softmax, which otherwise saturates on confident predictions) flow back to the last convolution layer to weight its feature maps. That layer is a 4 × 4 grid, so the heatmap is coarse. The resulting heatmap shows which regions influenced the score. It is an interpretability aid, not proof of biological cause.

{{ icon('shield') }}

Image checks

Before the disease model runs, images are checked for blur, darkness, overexposure and resolution (no dataset photo is refused), and a trained cauliflower-leaf validator (DINOv2 embedding + logistic regression) refuses anything that is not a cauliflower leaf. On a held-out test of 1,536 images it accepted none of 1,099 unrelated images and refused 2 of 437 cauliflower leaves.

{{ icon('compare') }} Visual similarity search

Each uploaded photo is also embedded with DINOv2-small, a general-purpose self-supervised vision model (run with ONNX Runtime), and compared by cosine similarity with an index of reference photos from the training and validation splits, using an exact FAISS inner-product index. Similarity measures visual closeness, not disease probability, and the embedding model was not trained on cauliflower diseases.

On the 99 test images (not in the index), the most similar reference had the same label 96 times. This overstates real-world performance: many test photos have other photos of the same plant in the reference set.

The search runs in a separate lightweight service (ONNX Runtime and FAISS, no TensorFlow), so it never slows down or breaks the disease analysis.

{{ icon('gauge') }} Visual severity estimate

The dataset has no lesion masks, so no segmentation model could be trained or validated. Instead, a colour-based method finds the leaf region (green to blue-green tissue, with enclosed holes filled) and counts yellow, tan and brown pixels inside it. The 0-10 / 10-30 / 30-60 / 60%+ bands are a display convention, not validated severity classes.

As a sanity check on the test split, healthy leaves had a median estimate of 2.7% and Downy Mildew 18.9%, but Black Rot (5.4%) is underestimated and photos of the cauliflower head are not estimated.

Known limitations

  • The test set is small ({{ m.test_images }} images, only {{ m.per_class['Black Rot'].support }} Black Rot), so each figure carries several points of uncertainty.
  • Training accuracy ({{ '%.1f'|format(m.train_accuracy * 100) }}%) is above validation ({{ '%.1f'|format(m.val_accuracy * 100) }}%), indicating moderate overfitting.
  • All images come from one dataset. Accuracy on other farms, cameras and lighting conditions is unknown.
  • The split keeps near-identical files together, but different photos of the same plant can be in both training and test sets. The test accuracy is therefore likely optimistic for photos of new plants.
  • The model always chooses one of four classes. Other diseases, pests or nutrient problems will be mapped to one of them.
  • The leaf check cannot distinguish a cauliflower leaf from other green leaves.

Responsible use

CauliCare provides image-based screening and is not a definitive agricultural diagnosis. Use it to decide what to look at more closely, and confirm important decisions such as removing plants or applying treatments with a local agricultural extension officer or plant-health laboratory.
